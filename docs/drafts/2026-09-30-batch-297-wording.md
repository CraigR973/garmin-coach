# Batch 297 — what Mark sees when the app reads his check-in note

**Status: signed off by Craig on Mark's behalf, 30 Sep 2026** (Mark is away until 6 Oct).
This is the copy that ships with Batch 297.

The app now reads his morning note once, the way he wrote it. What it finds can only
make the day more cautious. His own answers are the only thing that can make it less.

## 1. Home asks when his note might name a symptom

A card under the day's colour:

> **Any symptoms today?**
> Your note mentions “heartburn kept me awake”. If that’s a symptom, tell me, so
> today’s plan fits.
> **[Answer]** → opens his check-in, where the symptom question is

When the reader can't quote his words:

> Something in your note might be a symptom. If it is, tell me, so today’s plan fits.

It shows when the note hints at a symptom (a scratchy throat, heartburn, "caught a
chill", hay fever) or says he feels unwell (a hangover, an upset stomach). It also shows
when the note names a symptom outright, so he can correct the app if it misread him.
Once he answers, the card goes, and his answer decides the day.

## 2. When his note names a symptom

A sore throat, a fever, chest tightness and so on set the same floor as ticking that
answer on the check-in. **The notices are Batch 294's, unchanged** ("You've told me you
have a head cold …"), because he did tell the app. In the brief, the coach quotes his
words, for example:

> Your note says “woke with a sore throat and a runny nose”, so today is easy riding
> at most.

## 3. When his note says he feels unwell or unusually tired

How he feels counts one notch worse than his score alone. The "why" line on the day
quotes him:

> One thing is a little off: you wrote “legs like lead”.

## 4. When the note can't be read

If the reading fails (for example during an Anthropic billing outage), the colour comes
from his numbers and his answers, and the brief says so in one sentence:

> I couldn't read your note today, so today's colour comes from your numbers and your
> answers alone.

## 5. Decisions made on Mark's behalf (overrule any)

1. Home also asks when the note names a symptom outright, not only when it is unclear,
   so a misreading is one tap from being corrected.
2. Answering the check-in again after the note was read counts as his answer: a note's
   symptom no longer sets the floor.
3. Feeling unwell (a gut upset, a hangover) makes the day one notch more cautious, as
   unusual tiredness does. You confirmed this for the hangover; the gut upset follows
   the same rule.
4. Causes (drink, travel, a broken night, training load, a recovery week) never change
   the colour. The coach mentions one only when it explains his numbers, and they feed
   the two-Reds rule.
