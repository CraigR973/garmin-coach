# Wording to sign off — Part 3: when a Red becomes an Amber

Nothing here is live yet. "Fine as is", or say what you'd change.

---

## What is changing

On 23 and 24 Sep the app made the day Red because Garmin's **7-day** HRV average (43
and 44 ms) sat just under Garmin's floor (45). Your **overnight** HRV on those mornings
was 45 and 47, at or above the floor, and your recovery time, resting heart rate and
training load all said you were recovered.

The change makes **last night's reading** decide the day. When your overnight HRV is at
or above the floor but the 7-day average isn't, the day becomes **Amber** instead of
Red. On 24 Sep that would have meant 70 minutes with the work at 76%, instead of 47
minutes at 60%. Amber still shortens a session by a quarter and makes it one zone
easier. A night genuinely below the floor, like 21 and 22 Sep (37 and 39 ms), stays
Red.

## What the brief will say

It leads with your own numbers and gives the reason second.

**When the 7-day average is what's lagging:**

> Amber today, not Red. Your own overnight HRV was 47 ms, above Garmin's floor of 45,
> and your recovery time, resting heart rate and training load all say you're
> recovered. Garmin's 7-day average (44 ms) is still just under its band, so today's
> session is a bit shorter and one zone easier, not halved.

**When Garmin has moved its floor (as on 18 and 19 Sep):**

> Amber today, not Red. Last night was one of your best: sleep 95, overnight HRV 48 ms,
> readiness 78. Garmin moved its HRV floor up to 46 this morning. Your reading didn't
> change; the threshold did.

**Choices:**
- "a bit shorter and one zone easier, not halved", or give the minutes and power?
- Give the actual numbers, as above, or just "above/below the floor"?
