Mark, hope the holiday's going well. Nothing here needs anything from you. It's what has
changed while you've been away, so the app makes sense when you're back on Wednesday.

**How the colour works now.** The app looks at four things, each against your own normal:
your HRV and resting heart rate, your sleep, your training load, and how you say you feel.
Each is either fine, a little off, or clearly off.

- Nothing off: Green.
- One thing a little off: Green, hold your targets. Ride as planned without pushing past the
  top of each zone. If it's a hard day and there's a better day this week, it'll offer to
  swap.
- Two things a little off, or one clearly off: Amber. Only the hard intervals ease a zone,
  and the ride keeps its full length. Zone 2 rides stay exactly as planned.
- Two things clearly off, or you say you feel rough: Red.

Garmin's HRV status and band aren't used for the colour any more. Your HRV is your own
7-day average against your own normal, so a moved floor can't cut a session. Garmin
readiness doesn't vote on its own; it can only back up a sleep or load reading.

**Your 18–27 Sep mornings under the new rules:** 18 Sep Green; 19, 22, 23 and 26 Sep
Green, hold your targets (22 Sep's VO₂ would have moved or been ridden as planned); 24 Sep
Amber, so the sweet spot keeps its full length with the intervals eased a zone; 21, 25
and 27 Sep Amber. You were right about the sledgehammer.

**Your first two weeks back are lighter weeks in your plan** (W12 consolidation, then the
W13 taper). In those weeks, if something is a little or clearly off, today's session stays
as planned and you hold your targets. Only a Red morning or a symptom changes a session.

**New on the check-in: "Any symptoms today?"** None is ticked for you, so an ordinary
morning costs nothing. If you pick one:
- Head cold: the day shows Red, with easy riding at most and nothing hard.
- Fever or aches: no training until it's gone, then build back gently over a few easy days.
- Chest or heart (pain or tightness, a racing or irregular heartbeat, feeling faint): no
  training that day, and speak to your GP or 111. If you have chest pain right now, ring 999.

**The app now reads your check-in note.** It can only make a day more careful, never less.
If you write that you feel a bit off or unusually tired, that counts as one thing a little
off, the same as your feel score dropping. If the note mentions something that might be a
symptom, Home will ask you the symptom question; your answer decides it.

**This morning (1 Oct)** was Amber on a holiday rest day: last night's HRV was 38 against
your usual 47, and your note said you were a bit more tired. Two small things together make
Amber. Nothing to do on a rest day.

**Your heart-rate wording.** A small rise in resting heart rate is now "a little off" with
a calm note, no illness talk. Only a jump of 7 bpm or more says take the day off the bike,
and the GP line only applies if you also feel unwell.

**Your HRV hunch holds up.** Your overnight HRV averaged 45.3 ms in recovery weeks against
48.1 in build weeks. The app now judges taper-week HRV against your own recovery-week
normal, so the expected dip isn't read as strain. When you're back, "Look for new memories"
on the Coach memory page should offer to track it as an experiment.

**When a number looks wrong.** Under the brief, the weekly review and each ride there's a
collapsed "How these numbers were worked out". Open it to see how a figure was reached.
"This looks wrong" saves your note with the working you were shown, and I look at those.
"I disagree with today's call" is recorded and shows in your weekly review. Neither changes
the day by itself.

**The weekly review** now also shows your key sessions done against planned, and which
mornings were cautious and why, so we can both see if the app is cutting too much.

**Fixed since you raised them:** the strength read compares your heart rate with your own

12-second sprints no longer count as VO₂; a ride is no longer logged as a session you didn't
do; REM is described the same way on the brief and on Trends; the bedroom peak is measured
over the night, not the afternoon.
