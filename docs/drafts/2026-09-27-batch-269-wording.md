## The decision behind the words

| Question (25 Sep) | Answer, made for Mark |
|---|---|
| A day like 23 Sep: 7-day HRV just under the floor, everything else normal | b) the full session, with its targets held. The colour stays Green, because Amber in this app means a cut. |
| Which signals may ease a hard session on their own | All four, unchanged: resting heart rate up, low readiness, a poor night, feeling rough. Any one of them beside the HRV flag makes the day Amber. |

His own words on 23 Sep set the direction: last night's reading "should be recognised and
soften any change", and a day like that "should at worst be Amber".

## The words the app writes itself

These are deterministic: the first reason shows on the Sleep page's day card, and the plan
line goes to the brief.

**When the 7-day average is what's lagging (23 Sep):**

> Garmin's 7-day HRV average (43 ms) is under its 45 ms floor, but last night's own
> reading (45 ms) is not, and no other signal disagrees, so the HRV flag alone does not cut
> the session.

**When Garmin moved its floor (18 Sep):**

> Garmin moved its HRV floor to 46 ms from a usual 44.5 ms while last night's reading held
> at 48 ms, and no other signal disagrees, so the moved floor alone does not cut the session.

**The plan line on a hold morning:**

> Hold the prescribed targets rather than pushing past the top of each zone.

## What the brief is told

It leads with his own measured recovery and its numbers (last night's HRV against the
floor, then the other signals that agree), says the session stands as planned with its
targets held, and only then gives the mechanism: the lagging 7-day average, or the floor
Garmin moved. It never calls a hold morning cut, eased or cautious because of HRV. On an
eased morning it names what else disagreed.
