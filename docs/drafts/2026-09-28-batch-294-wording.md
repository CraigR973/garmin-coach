# Batch 294 — the symptom question, its notices, and the calmer HRV line

**Status: draft, for Craig to sign off on Mark's behalf** (Mark is on holiday until 6 Oct).
No clinician check (Craig, 28 Sep). Batch 294 does not merge until this is signed off.

Example figures are Mark's own: 22 Sep (39 ms against a usual 47 ms) and 16 Jul (33 ms
against 48 ms).

## 1. The question on the morning check-in

It sits under "How you feel today", in the same card.

> **Any symptoms today?**
>
> - **None**
> - **Head cold** — runny or blocked nose, sneezing, sore throat
> - **Fever or aches** — a temperature, aching muscles, or a chesty cough
> - **Chest or heart** — chest pain or tightness, a racing or irregular heartbeat, or feeling faint

**None is preselected**, so an ordinary morning costs him nothing. A morning with no
check-in has no answer, and the app never treats that as "None".

## 2. The notices

They appear in the notice card on Home and on the brief. The heading comes from Batch 293's
levels.

**Chest or heart** — heading "Why this needs rest":

> You've told me about chest pain, a racing or irregular heartbeat, or feeling faint. That
> needs a doctor's view before any training, so no training of any kind today. Speak to your
> GP or call 111 today. If you have chest pain right now, call 999.

**Fever or aches** — heading "Why this needs rest":

> You've told me about a fever, aches or a chest infection. Training through that adds
> strain your body doesn't need and usually makes it last longer, so no training of any kind
> until the symptoms have gone. When you're clear, build back gradually: a few easy days
> before anything hard.

**Head cold** — heading "Why today is capped":

> You've told me you have a head cold — symptoms above the neck. Easy riding at most today,
> and only if you feel up to it: nothing hard, no intervals and no VO₂. If it moves below the
> neck — a fever, aches or a chesty cough — stop training until you're clear.

"At most" is deliberate: if another signal takes him off the bike the same morning, the two
notices still agree.

## 3. The headline on a no-training morning

Home and the brief already swap the verdict headline for "Take today off the bike" when a
signal rules riding out. A chest-or-heart or fever answer gets its own:

> **No training today**
> The symptoms you've reported rule out training today.

## 4. The overnight-HRV notices

**The milder dip (new).** Keeps the Amber cap and loses the off-the-bike instruction and
the illness and GP talk, as Batch 293 did for resting heart rate. Heading "Why today is
capped":

> Your overnight HRV is 39 ms this morning against a usual 47 ms — lower than most nights
> for you. Dips like this usually come from a short night, a drink, a busy week or a hard
> day before. On its own it caps today at Amber: an eased session, not a day off the bike.

**A dip that comes with a second sign (new).** Off the bike, heading "Why this needs rest".
The middle clause names what came with it:

> Your overnight HRV is 39 ms this morning against a usual 47 ms, and your resting heart
> rate is up as well. Either on its own would only cap the day; together they are worth
> respecting. Take today off the bike. If you feel unwell, rest until it settles, and see
> your GP if it doesn't.

The alternatives for the middle clause: "and you've told me you have a head cold", "and
you've told me about a fever, aches or a chest infection", "and you've told me about chest
or heart symptoms".

**The illness-grade drop (unchanged).** Only 16 Jul in three months:

> Your overnight HRV is 33 ms this morning against a usual 48 ms … — a drop that size in a
> single night is unusual for you. It usually means one of: an infection starting, a heavy
> drink, a badly broken night, or real stress carried into sleep. Training hard through it
> tends to deepen it. Take today off the bike, and if you feel unwell alongside it, see
> your GP rather than just resting.

## 5. "How these numbers were worked out" — one new row

The HRV floor's working (Batch 273) gains one row under "Rule":

> **Off the bike:** only at 34.6 ms or lower (2.5 standard deviations or 30% under your
> median), or below the floor together with a raised resting heart rate or a symptom
> you've reported.

## 6. Lines the coach may quote

These go into the brief's data, not onto a screen. The coach quotes reasons when asked
why.

**Reasons:**

- Chest or heart: "Reported chest pain, a racing or irregular heartbeat, or faintness sets
  a Red floor: no training of any kind today."
- Fever or aches: "Reported fever, aches or a chest infection sets a Red floor: no training
  of any kind until the symptoms have gone."
- Head cold: "A reported head cold sets a Red floor: easy riding at most, with nothing hard
  and no VO2."

**Plan lines:**

- Chest or heart: "No training of any kind today — not the bike, strength or mobility work."
- Fever or aches: "No training of any kind until the symptoms have gone, then build back
  gradually."
- Head cold: the app's existing Red lines. A Zone 2 ride stays Zone 2, shorter; anything
  harder becomes an easy recovery spin; no VO₂.

## 7. Decisions made on Mark's behalf (overrule any)

1. None is preselected.
2. A head cold is Red on the easy path: the app's existing Red ride changes, nothing new.
3. Chest-or-heart and fever mornings offer no ride at all, and a chest-or-heart morning
   drops the breathwork suggestion too.
4. A symptom answer plus an HRV dip takes him off the bike.
5. The illness-grade HRV wording is unchanged.

## What his last three months would have been

The HRV rule told him to stay off the bike on 6 of 75 mornings. With this batch it would
have said so once, on 16 Jul. The colours do not change: every one of those mornings keeps
its Amber cap, and 21 and 22 Sep stay Red for their other reason. On those Red mornings the
app would have offered the usual Red ride instead of no ride.
