## Blocking findings

### 1. 269.1 names the wrong corroborating signal, and the spec contradicts itself

269.1 says: *"Require corroboration from `_acute_physiology_rail`."*
271.3 says: *"269's corroboration gate consumes 271's recalibration signal."*

These are different gates with very different behaviour, and only one is correct.

The acute rail is an **acute** detector: overnight HRV below `median − 1.5 × SD`
over an 84-day rolling window, minimum 21 samples. Gate the Red behind it and two
things break:

- **Tier collapse.** `requiresBikeRest` is `rhr.triggered or hrv.triggered`. If Red
  now requires the rail, every HRV-driven Red also carries *"take today off the
  bike, and see your GP if you feel unwell alongside it."* There is no longer an
  intermediate "ease it hard" state — only Amber or medical escalation.
- **Structural blindness to slow decline.** The rail's baseline is a rolling
  median. A gradual HRV suppression drags the median down with it and can never
  produce a 1.5-SD single-night drop. That is precisely the overreaching
  signature, and it would become invisible to the Red path.

**Fix:** strike the rail from 269.1. 271's recalibration signal is the right gate,
which is what the group note already assumes.

### 2. 269 needs two changes, and the root cause is not the one named

`_hrv_below_baseline` reads `hrv_weekly_avg_ms or hrv_last_night_avg_ms` — the
**seven-day average is primary** and this morning's actual measurement is only a
fallback. That is why 18 Sep was Red on a 48 ms night: the app was reading a
number from last week.

Checked across 10–22 Sep production:

| Change | 18 Sep | 19 Sep | 21/22 Sep |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overnight reading primary | ✅ fixed | ✗ still Red (45 vs 46) | ✅ still Red |
| 271 recalibration gate only | ✅ fixed | ✅ fixed | ✅ still Red |
| Both | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ still Red |

Neither alone is sufficient for both days; the row names only one and it is not
the load-bearing one.

**Related, and worth saying in the decision record:** `hrv_low and hrv_status in
{unbalanced, low}` is not two signals. `hrv_status` *is* Garmin's own weekly-average-
versus-band comparison, and `_hrv_below_baseline` recomputes that same comparison
from the same three columns. The unconditional Red is one vendor signal counted
twice and presented as corroboration.

**Also verified, so 269.2 can stop hedging:** with line 810 gated, 18 Sep does land
on the Amber at line 826. `soft_sleep_override` sits between them and could have
stolen it to Green, but it requires `hrv_status in {balanced, …}` and
`not hrv_below_baseline`, so it cannot fire on an unbalanced morning. The
recommendation holds.

### 3. 268.3's stated premise is factually false, and it changes the test plan

The row says corrected peaks are 17.9–19.7 °C, so the disruption count *"goes to
zero and stays there … which makes the signal untestable."*

On the real 21:00–07:00 window, **21 Sep peaked at 20.51 °C** — over the threshold
— and 14 Sep reached 19.94. The signal is live, not degenerate.

The recommendation (leave `THERMAL_DISRUPTION_C` at 20.0) is still right, but the
justification must go, and 268.4 needs a positive fixture: **15–21 Sep must report
exactly one disruption night, not zero and not five.** A test suite that only ever
asserts zero cannot tell a working detector from a dead one.

Worth noting alongside: 21 Sep is one of the two genuine Reds. A genuinely warm
bedroom on the night his HRV hit 37 ms is a real coaching signal the current bug
buries, because today the app cries "disrupted" every single night.

Seasonality, for the record: monthly indoor means run 22.0 (Jun) → 20.8 (Jul) →
19.6 (Aug) → 18.9 (Sep). A fixed 20.0 °C threshold will fire often in summer and
rarely in winter. Not a blocker, but do not be surprised by it in July.

### 4. 275.1 would ship a statistically invalid comparator

`GROUP_THRESHOLD = 3.0` carries the comment `# age-adjusted sleep points = a
meaningful gap`. Applied to HRV in milliseconds, **3.0 ms is inside Mark's
night-to-night noise** — his September overnight values run 37–52 on a ~45 median.
With `GROUP_MIN_PER_GROUP = 7`, a bare difference of means, no variance, no
confidence interval and no significance test, the evaluator would return
**`supported` from noise**.

That is worse than today's careful refusal. Today the app declines to claim
something it cannot test; after 275.1 as written it would manufacture confidence
about his physiology and put it in a coaching packet.

Second defect: **there is no polarity field.** `evaluate_group_compare` hardcodes
"recovery lower than build ⇒ supported". An experiment on resting HR — where lower
is better — would return exactly the wrong verdict.

**275.1 must carry, per metric: the threshold, the direction, and a variance-aware
statistic** (Welch's t, or a standardised effect size with a CI). Without those
three, do not ship it.

## The clinical read

The wave calls 18/19 Sep an artifact. Before suppressing anything, the record
should say what the fortnight actually looks like, because "artifact" is only half
of it.

**Seven-day HRV average fell 49 → 43 across 10–22 Sep — a monotonic 12% decline.**

Against that:

| Signal | 5–22 Sep |
|---|---|
| Resting HR | flat, 43–45 throughout |
| Respiration | flat, 11–12 |
| SpO₂ | flat, 96–97 |
| Sleep (age-adjusted) | 74–95, good |
| Acute load | **halved**, 630 → 305 |
| Readiness | mostly HIGH |

**This is not overreaching.** Overreaching moves resting HR and load together and
degrades sleep; here load is falling by half and RHR has not moved a beat. **It is
not an illness pattern either** — SpO₂ and respiration are the two things a
respiratory infection moves, and both are flat.

The most defensible reading is ordinary baseline drift (Garmin's own band moved
down too: high 56 → 55 on 20 Sep) plus a discrete stressor on 21 Sep, where
`stress_avg` hit 29 against a 14–18 baseline and the bedroom genuinely reached
20.5 °C. **So suppressing 18/19 Sep is clinically defensible.** Good.

But two consequences follow:

- **270 should record the decline, not silently discount it.** If it only
  decrements a counter, the app has taught itself to ignore the run-up — and the
  next time this pattern *does* precede something real, the leading indicator is
  gone. Emit the classification per day in the packet (the row already asks for
  this) *and* keep the trend visible.
- **269.3's copy should not lead with the moved floor.** The far stronger argument
  is that **18 Sep was Mark's best night of the fortnight**: 95 age-adjusted sleep,
  7.3 h, overnight HRV 48 — his highest in six days — readiness 78 HIGH, RHR 44,
  acute load 452 in a recovery week. Every independent signal said recovered. That
  is much more convincing to him than "Garmin moved a number", and it is true.

## Per-batch notes

**268 — good, buildable now.** Both functions already carry Batch 235's
`temperature_series_columns()` projection; the shared leaf must keep it, and the
new sleep-window lookup must project `sleep_start_utc`/`sleep_end_utc` rather than
`select(Sleep)`. Add the new call site to Batch 235's SQL-recording test or the
egress guard silently stops covering it. Fix 268.3 per finding 3.

**271 — correct, and correctly moved ahead of 269.** The phase-pinning instinct is
right, and I confirmed why: 17–22 Sep, every HRV field is identical across
`morning` and `settled`, and only `readiness_score` differs (e.g. 18 Sep: 78 vs
79). So an unpinned detector is harmless *today* and would be wrong the first day
it is not. Pin it.

**269 — do not build as written.** Findings 1 and 2.

**270 — sound, but its value expires on 26 Sep.** The artifact days age out of the
rolling window that day. The recommendation to give the artifact classification its
own exclusion budget rather than raising the shared one is right and I'd hold it:
the existing caps bound *explained* Reds where the strain was real; this bounds
*mis-measured* ones where it was not. Note that `_qualify_red_morning` only reaches
the exclusion paths at all when a check-in reason exists — a band-artifact Red has
none, so the new classification needs its own branch before the
`systemic_markers_strained` fallthrough, not a tweak to `hrv_crashed`.

**272 — the weakest-specified batch of the eight.** The cross-surface agreement
check is a genuinely general idea and worth building. "Structural plausibility" is
not: all three examples given are restatements of the 268 bug, and a gate that only
catches the bug you just fixed is a regression test wearing a gate's clothes.
**Recommend: ship 272 as cross-surface agreement only, and move the plausibility
examples into 268's own test file.** The mark-and-alert recommendation over
blocking is right.

**273 — high value, keep it.** One gap: "a derived figure without provenance fails
a contract test" needs a mechanism. The test cannot know what "derived" means
without a registry the packet builders must register into. Specify that at
`/batch-start` or the contract test will be vacuous.

**274 — split it.** 274.4 (dissent record that changes nothing) is cheap, safe and
good. 274.2 (a contest suppressing a deterministic input) is a new rail plus a
migration plus a Mark-facing UI plus an admin alert path that is still dormant
(`admin_alert_user_id` is unset in production). **Recommend: build 274.4 with 273,
and hold 274.2 until 273 has been in front of Mark for a fortnight** — then you
will know which figures he actually presses, rather than guessing.

**275 — right instinct, right to show him first, but fix finding 4.** The
"reject at creation any hypothesis that binds to no evaluator" half is excellent
and independent of the statistics problem; it could ship on its own. The
"Mark proposes, Craig confirms" recommendation is right.
